Key findings

Payment behaviour dominates credit limits. Repayment status in September (PAY_0) has the strongest association with default (Cramér's V = 0.42, χ² = 5,363); credit-limit correlation is only −0.15.
Delinquency compounds risk fast. Clients paid duly in Sep default at 16.8%; a 2-month delay explodes the rate to 69.1% — a 4.1× jump from a single status code.
Calibration makes probabilities bankable. Isotonic calibration moves Brier 0.135 → 0.134 and aligns predicted vs observed default rates within ~1 point in every risk band.

Threshold sweep — the cost of catching defaulters

Calibrated XGBoost on the hold-out test set · vertical line = chosen operating point 0.26

Why 0.26?

The threshold was tuned to maximise F1 on the validation set (not the test set), then applied to the test set exactly once — no test-set peeking.
At 0.26 the model catches 58.2% of all defaulters at 50.8% precision — in a 22%-prevalence portfolio that is a 2.3× lift over random selection.
Isotonic calibration was selected over sigmoid (Platt) by validation Brier score (0.1340 vs 0.1352).

What drives risk? — SHAP global importance

Mean |SHAP value| — average impact on prediction magnitude · TreeExplainer on the tuned XGBoost

The single strongest signal

ON_TIME_COUNT_6M — the 6-month on-time payment streak — dominates every other feature (mean |SHAP| 0.97, ~4× the runner-up). Recent behaviour beats demographics, limits and balances.
PAY_0 (current-month repayment status) is the strongest raw feature; engineered delinquency aggregates (MAX_DELINQUENCY_6M, WEIGHTED_DELINQUENCY) add further signal.
Demographics (SEX, MARRIAGE, EDUCATION) appear in the top 5 by magnitude but are an order weaker than behaviour — and are audited separately in the Fairness tab.

Fairness audit — performance by group

TPR = share of defaulters caught · FPR = share of good clients flagged · test set n = 4,495

Reading the audit honestly

Observed performance gaps are modest. TPR differs by ~2 points between genders (57.4% vs 59.1%); the age bands 21–30 and 46+ are within 0.1 points of each other.
Selection rate tracks base rates. Males are flagged more often (29.2% vs 22.8%) — and males in this dataset do default more (24.8% vs 20.4% observed). The model is reacting to behaviour, not identity.
These are diagnostics, not verdicts. Differences in a research dataset do not by themselves establish discriminatory impact in a real lending institution — but monitoring them should be part of any deployment.
